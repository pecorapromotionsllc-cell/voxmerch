## Tuesday — Custom Skills That Persist Across Sessions

### Post 2: LinkedIn
- **Topic**: The concept of reusable AI skills/commands for repeatable workflows
- **Visual**: No
- **Template**: N/A
- **Draft**:

Every content creator has a process they repeat.

Research a topic. Write a draft. Format it for the platform. Add a visual. Hit publish.

The problem with AI tools is you end up re-explaining this process every single time you start a new session.

There's a better approach. Build it once as a reusable skill.

A skill is a set of instructions, API calls, and formatting rules packaged into one command. It lives in your project. It persists across sessions.

Type one command. The entire workflow runs.

No re-prompting. No copy-pasting old conversations. No starting from scratch.

If you find yourself giving AI the same instructions more than twice, turn it into a skill.

## Wednesday — Automated Quality Checks Before Content Goes Live

### Post 3: LinkedIn
- **Topic**: Quality gates and automated pre-publish checks for content consistency
- **Visual**: No
- **Template**: N/A
- **Draft**:

AI-generated content has a tell.

Em dashes everywhere. Overused buzzwords. A tone that doesn't match your brand.

Most people catch these issues manually. They read every post, fix the same patterns, and hope nothing slips through.

There's a more reliable way. Automate the quality check.

Set up rules that run before anything gets published.

Flag banned words your brand avoids. Remove formatting patterns you don't want. Check character limits. Verify tone against your brand guidelines.

Every post goes through the same filter. Every time. Without you having to remember.

The best content process isn't one that produces faster. It's one that produces consistently.

## Thursday — Batch Content Creation and Scheduling

### Post 4: LinkedIn
- **Topic**: The power of sitting down once per week to batch-create and schedule everything
- **Visual**: No
- **Template**: N/A
- **Draft**:

The most productive content creators I know spend about two hours per week on social media.

Not two hours per day. Two hours total.

Here's how. They batch everything.

Sit down once. Feed in your topics for the week. Let AI research, draft, and format each post. Review the full plan. Make your edits. Approve. Schedule everything to your content calendar.

Five days of content. Handled in one sitting.

The posts queue into your next available slots. Monday through Friday fills up automatically.

No more scrambling at 8am wondering what to post. No more "I'll get to it later" turning into silence on your feed.

Batch production isn't about being lazy. It's about being intentional with your time.

## Friday — Keeping Your Brand Voice Consistent at Scale

### Post 5: LinkedIn
- **Topic**: How to maintain authentic brand voice when using AI for content
- **Visual**: No
- **Template**: N/A
- **Draft**:

The number one concern with AI content is always the same.

"It doesn't sound like me."

And that's valid. Default AI output sounds generic. It leans on the same phrases, the same structure, the same tone.

But generic output is a configuration problem, not a fundamental limit.

Here's what changes the output.

Feed in samples of your best-performing posts. The ones that got engagement. The ones that felt right. Give AI your specific voice rules. Words you never use. Sentence patterns you prefer. How you open a post versus how you close one.

The AI stops guessing and starts matching.

Your voice isn't something AI replaces. It's something AI learns to replicate when you give it the right inputs.

The more specific your guidelines, the less editing you do after.
